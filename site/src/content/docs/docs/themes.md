This page shows you how to change how Penmoss looks: light or dark, the theme, the fonts and the width of your notes.

Most of it is in **Settings → Appearance** (**Appearance and Themes** in the command palette). It has two tabs, as **Settings → Sidebar** and **Settings → Note** do, and as it has on Android. **Settings**, which it opens on, holds the settings in three groups, **Theme**, **Text** and, on the Mac, **Window**. **Preview** shows Penmoss itself as large as the page allows, with your sidebar and the note you opened last, and a line naming the theme and whether it is light or dark. It changes as you change the mode, the theme, the fonts or the text size, and nothing in it takes a click of its own. Under it, **Fonts at Real Size** shows your text at the size Penmoss draws it: the interface's page titles, sidebar, settings, body and help text, and a note's title, headings, text and code, each labelled with what it is, its font and its size (for example, Text · Inter · 16 px). It changes as you change a font or the size. On a narrow window the preview shows the note as a phone does. On Android the preview is Home's own row of shortcuts above a note, with the interface's text at its real size under it. The theme editor's preview and a notebook's badge preview are beside their settings instead: click one (or press Return on it), where a magnifying glass shows as you point at it, to see it larger in a window of its own; **Close**, Escape or a click outside it closes it. The mode, theme, fonts and text size you pick are kept on that device only, Mac or phone, and never reach your other devices ([Kept on each device](/docs/reference/settings/#kept-on-each-device)). Your custom themes are on every device. What is marked **This Mac** stays on that Mac.

## Light and dark

**Mode** is **Light**, **Dark** or **System**, which follows your Mac's or phone's appearance (the default). **Toggle Dark Mode** switches between light and dark from the command palette.

## Themes

Penmoss comes with eight themes, each in a light and a dark version: **Penmoss** (the default), **Paper**, **Slate**, **Nord**, **Solarized**, **Gruvbox**, **Rosé Pine** and **High Contrast**. Pick one under **Theme**, or use **Switch Theme…** from the command palette.

### Your own theme

The built-in themes cannot be changed, but you can copy one and change the copy:

1. Choose a theme and click **Customize** under the themes (it reads **Customize Nord…**, for example). For a built-in theme, Penmoss makes a copy named "… copy", chooses it and opens the **Theme Editor** on it. For a theme of your own, it opens the editor on that theme.
2. Change its name, its light and dark colours, and under **Type and Layout** its interface, note and code fonts and its note width (32 to 72 rem). The preview beside the editor shows the variant you are editing as you change it, with its fonts at their real size under it.
3. Changes save as you make them. **← Appearance** goes back.

If a pair of colours is too close for comfortable reading (below WCAG AA contrast), the editor says which. **Delete This Theme** deletes it on every device.

## Text

Under **Text**:

- **Interface Font** and **Note Font**: Theme Default, System, Serif, Rounded, Humanist, Monospace, or **Custom…** with the name of a font installed on the device. A font that is not installed falls back to the system font. Code always uses the theme's monospace font.
- **Note Font Size**: 12 to 28 px, 16 by default on a Mac and 18 on Android. The preview shows the size as you drag. **Reset** puts it back.
- **Show Tooltips After**: how long the pointer rests on a button before its name shows: 0.5, 1 (the default), 1.5 or 2 seconds, or **Never**. Moving to a button with the Tab key shows its name at once.
- **Dad Jokes**: a dad joke as the heading of a window with no note open. On by default; turn it off and the heading reads **Welcome to Penmoss**. The two lines on where to start stay either way.

## Note width

**Settings → Note → Note Width** is the theme's width, about 52 rem, or **Full Window**. **Toggle Full-Width Notes** (⌘⌥⇧W, or the button at the note's top right) switches between them. The rest of how a note is laid out is on the same page; see [The note page in Settings](/docs/organise/#the-note-in-settings).

## On the Mac

**Settings → Appearance → Window → Compact Title Bar** (on by default, **This Mac**) puts the window's buttons in Penmoss's own top row, so notes start higher. Turn it off for macOS's own title bar; Penmoss asks, then reopens its windows where they were. See [The Mac app](/docs/install/mac/#the-compact-title-bar).
